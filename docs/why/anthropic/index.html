Why Anthropic

Anthropic is shaping enterprise AI, and I want to grow with it

In a fast-growing organization, each person's scope and the problems they own grow with it. Anthropic is growing across every industry. By focusing on models that can complete enterprise work end to end, it has increased its share of enterprise LLM spend from 12% to 40% in two years and now leads the enterprise AI market.

Korea is one of the best markets to test this shift quickly. It is digitally advanced, quick to adopt new technology, and among the top dozen countries in Claude usage. With Korea's largest conglomerates now rolling out Claude across their groups, the standards for enterprise AI in Korea are being set now, and I want to help set them.

The tools keep changing, but problems still need solving, and more of them now come to Anthropic

What I bring is problem solving. The shape of a problem varies by industry, and AI models keep improving; what is right today may not be right in a year. What endures is the judgment of which problem to solve and how to approach it most effectively. I have seen this hold across engagements in consumer electronics, smartphones, steel, auto parts and higher education.

What has changed is implementation. Even when recent projects were framed with traditional consulting methods, execution increasingly required a data platform and AI capabilities. As a result, enterprise problems are converging on the companies that build AI. The work enterprises bring to Claude now spans coding, documents and finance, and Anthropic sees more of these problems, across more industries, earlier than anyone else. The Applied AI Architect role turns a client's business problem into something Claude can solve and carries it through to deployment, and that is the work I have done across industries: structuring client problems and driving decisions with data.

AI already lets me do work I couldn't do before

Delivering with Claude requires first-hand knowledge of what AI can do. Across client work and side projects, I have used it to do things that were previously out of reach.

  • Reconstructing the data model: On the CLV engagement we had no direct access to the client's database, so we maintained our analysis queries without knowing which tables existed or how they related. By giving AI the business context, I produced entity-relationship diagrams and data flow maps that let both the client and our team understand the data structure at a glance.
  • Keeping the team consistent: For the same reason, the whole team needed to work from identical definitions. I codified formulas, customer classification rules and past AI errors as shared context, so every analyst worked from the same definitions.
  • Testing market demand: For a pre-wedding party platform, I built the landing page, custom quote pages and a Claude API customer chatbot myself, without a developer, cutting the time from idea to market feedback to one tenth.

Using AI across analysis, team operations and product development has shown me how models behave within real data and systems. I now want to bring that experience to Korean enterprises and work with clients to expand the range of problems AI can solve.

Outside work, I build and run things people show up for

2026 · Pre-wedding party platform

Turning twenty separate pre-wedding dinners into one party

Visit the live site ↗

What

A service that plans and runs one group party for couples before their wedding. The first one was a rooftop wine bar party for a friend's couple: a team quiz about the bride and groom, tacos from the restaurant they went to as students, and one photo board instead of flowers. I then built the landing page, the sign-up form linked to Google Sheets, custom quote pages, and a Claude API chatbot for customer inquiries.

Why

A friend getting married had met people 25 separate times just to hand out invitations, more than 100 hours in total. At another friend's group party, simple team games got guests who had never met talking to each other.

What I learned

Check demand before building more. I opened a landing page, interviewed people who had done this, and ran one pilot before fixing packages and prices. For the chatbot, deciding what it must never say came first: partner venue names are kept out of the prompt entirely and blocked again by an output filter.

2026 · PAUZE TRIP

An outdoor, phone-free community trip combining hiking, a book club and a morning barre session

What

I ran an offline community built around outdoor activity. Participants met at dawn and travelled together by chartered bus, climbed the snow-covered Taebaeksan with phones switched off, held a book club in the evening, and closed the next morning with a singing-bowl meditation and barre class run with partner instructors.

Why

After reading The Comfort Crisis, I climbed Hallasan alone and spent a temple stay with my phone off. The next morning a monk was eating breakfast while watching short videos. I wanted to share the experience of filling screen time with nature, exercise and real conversation.

What I learned

The 160,000 won fee ran at a loss even with donated food, so the next price comes from a cost simulation. A phone-free hike still needs group leaders with phones for safety. People from different fields only started talking once the sign-up form asked them to describe their work in plain words.
