Why Palantir

Palantir built its platform where a wrong decision costs the most

Palantir started by building software for U.S. defense and intelligence agencies, where acting on the wrong data can cost lives. The ontology sits at the center of that approach: one model of an organization's data, logic and actions that both people and AI can work from. Palantir has since taken the same approach to companies through Foundry and AIP. In 2026 the U.S. Army folded 75 separate contracts into a single enterprise agreement with Palantir, and Palantir's U.S. commercial revenue more than doubled year over year. Few companies have proven this in both government and industry, and I want to help bring it to Korean enterprises.

AI helps a business only when it is connected to how the business decides

Palantir's post "Connecting AI to Decisions with the Palantir Ontology" says AI is useful to a business only when it is connected to real data, the logic behind it, and the actions people take. My last project showed me why. An auto parts manufacturer wanted an early warning system for profit, and most of the work came before any AI: what data the company had, how it was collected, and how it tied to profit. We mapped 309 cost accounts and found ten that explained 70.8% of the margin variance.

The CEO's chatbot showed me what happens without an ontology

The CEO was already asking an AI chatbot about reports, one file at a time, and kept quoting its answer from an old report even after we brought a newer one. Without a model of the business underneath, the chatbot could only talk about whatever file it was given. That was the moment I understood why the ontology matters.

A Deployment Strategist does the work I have been doing as a consultant

For five years I have defined client problems in business terms and designed the data behind the answer.

  • Reconstructing the data model: On a customer lifetime value engagement we had no direct access to the client's database. By giving AI the business context, I rebuilt the data model so the client and our team could see how their data connected.
  • Designing data for a new platform: For a global smartphone manufacturer's U.S. D2C platform, I defined target data structures, source-to-target mapping and purchase-journey data flows.
  • Testing market demand: For a pre-wedding party service, I built the landing page, sign-up form and a customer chatbot myself, without a developer.

I want to do this work full time with Korean enterprises, on a platform built to connect their data to their decisions.

Accounts were compared by how much they moved, not by their amounts. Production bonuses are a third the size of machinery depreciation, yet contribute four times as much to margin variance.

Why the analysis targeted margin: monthly profit, 2021–2025
Hold margin at its five-year average and the monthly profit line goes almost flat

Production profit by month, KRW billions. Actual profit swung by 1.4bn a month (one standard deviation); with margin held at its 0.9% average, revenue changes alone would have moved it by 0.04bn. So the analysis targeted margin.

Outside work, I build and run things people show up for

2026 · Pre-wedding party platform

Turning twenty separate pre-wedding dinners into one party

Visit the live site ↗

What

A service that plans and runs one group party for couples before their wedding. The first one was a rooftop wine bar party for a friend's couple: a team quiz about the bride and groom, tacos from the restaurant they went to as students, and one photo board instead of flowers. I then built the landing page, the sign-up form linked to Google Sheets, custom quote pages, and a Claude API chatbot for customer inquiries.

Why

A friend getting married had met people 25 separate times just to hand out invitations, more than 100 hours in total. At another friend's group party, simple team games got guests who had never met talking to each other.

What I learned

Check demand before building more. I opened a landing page, interviewed people who had done this, and ran one pilot before fixing packages and prices. For the chatbot, deciding what it must never say came first: partner venue names are kept out of the prompt entirely and blocked again by an output filter.

2026 · PAUZE TRIP

An outdoor, phone-free community trip combining hiking, a book club and a morning barre session

What

I ran an offline community built around outdoor activity. Participants met at dawn and travelled together by chartered bus, climbed the snow-covered Taebaeksan with phones switched off, held a book club in the evening, and closed the next morning with a singing-bowl meditation and barre class run with partner instructors.

Why

After reading The Comfort Crisis, I climbed Hallasan alone and spent a temple stay with my phone off. The next morning a monk was eating breakfast while watching short videos. I wanted to share the experience of filling screen time with nature, exercise and real conversation.

What I learned

The 160,000 won fee ran at a loss even with donated food, so the next price comes from a cost simulation. A phone-free hike still needs group leaders with phones for safety. People from different fields only started talking once the sign-up form asked them to describe their work in plain words.
